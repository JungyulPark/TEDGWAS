Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript, titled **"Druggable-gene-wide Mendelian randomization distinguishes TSHR-anchored susceptibility from IGF1R effector biology in Graves disease and thyroid eye disease"**, for consideration for publication in the *Journal of Endocrinological Investigation* as an Original Article.

This manuscript represents a completely restructured and refocused study, previously scoped for *Thyroid* or the *European Thyroid Journal*, that has been refined to concentrate strictly on the genetic triangulation of druggable targets (*TSHR*, *IGF1R*, and *CTLA4*) using high-rigor Mendelian randomization, colocalization, and target-relevant orbital tissue expression.

Thyroid eye disease (TED) is a debilitating extrathyroidal complication of Graves’ disease (GD). The clinical introduction of teprotumumab, a monoclonal antibody blocking the insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R), has revolutionized TED management. However, a critical translational question has remained unanswered: does *IGF1R* act as an independent genetic driver of disease risk, or does it represent a downstream pharmacologic effector target? 

To address this, we developed a systematic, druggable-gene-wide Mendelian randomization (MR) and colocalization framework integrating genetic association, statistical fine-mapping, and target-relevant orbital tissue expression. We analyzed a strict outcome hierarchy of decreasing sample size but increasing TED specificity: Biobank Japan Graves’ disease as primary discovery, Pan-UK Biobank hyperthyroidism as replication, and FinnGen R12 Graves’ ophthalmopathy (the largest available TED-specific GWAS) as a sensitivity outcome. We triangulated these genetic findings with exploratory bulk RNA-sequencing of TED and control orbital tissue from a clinical cohort.

Our study reports several novel findings of direct relevance to endocrinologists and ophthalmic researchers:
1. **TSHR as a Susceptibility Anchor:** *TSHR* showed robust, protective-direction MR effects across all three outcomes, which colocalized strongly with both discovery and TED-specific signals ($PP.H4 = 0.951$ and $0.986$) at a single lead variant (rs179252). Fine-mapping confirmed this as a locus-level anchor. This genetic signal aligned with significant upregulation of *TSHR* in TED orbital tissue ($\log_2\text{FC} = +2.33$, adjusted $P = 0.032$).
2. **IGF1R as an Effector Axis:** In contrast, *IGF1R* showed nominal positive-direction MR effects but completely lacked expression-colocalization support ($PP.H4 \le 0.043$; H2-dominant, outcome-only association) and showed no significant orbital tissue differential expression. This profile indicates that *IGF1R* is not a primary genetic driver of disease risk, but rather acts as a pharmacologic effector axis, reconciling its clinical tractability with its lack of independent genetic susceptibility.
3. **Rigorous Filtering to Prevent False Positives:** Out of 2,545 testable druggable genes, we applied stringent cross-outcome replication and colocalization criteria. While candidates like *TNFSF14* and *IFNGR1* colocalized in discovery, they failed in the TED outcome. Our study concludes that no non-known candidate survives this multi-layered filtering. We present this absence of novel targets as an informative, conservative result that prevents the overinterpretation of single-outcome MR signals.
4. **CTLA4 Calibration:** We included *CTLA4* as a positive autoimmune control, recovering its expected protective signal but demonstrating complex, outcome-dependent colocalization ($PP.H4 = 0.978$ in FinnGen, but $PP.H3$-dominant in BBJ), reflecting cross-ancestry locus heterogeneity.

This work directly reframes the relationship between disease susceptibility genes and therapeutic targets in thyroid autoimmunity. It provides a robust, reviewer-proof genetic template for evaluating current and future biologics in GD and TED, making it highly relevant to the readers of the *Journal of Endocrinological Investigation*. 

This manuscript is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the manuscript, and there are no conflicts of interest to disclose.
